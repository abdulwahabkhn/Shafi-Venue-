# Design System: Shafi Complex & Marquee

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Courtyard Axis"**

The system turns the venue's real architecture into interface structure. Near-black lacquer frames the experience, courtyard greens establish a locally grounded sense of place, and muted ceremonial gold traces alignment, thresholds, and decisive actions. Authentic exterior imagery remains the primary visual evidence; conceptual service imagery is treated as an explicitly disclosed planning direction inside a warm, paper-like ledger.

The character is assured, warm, and exact. Large humanist sans-serif statements create contemporary authority, while restrained supporting copy and squared controls keep the journey direct. Density is editorial rather than sparse: broad image fields and generous section rhythm are counterbalanced by compact ledgers, fine separators, clear enquiry details, and responsive typography that tightens without losing presence.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Authentic venue architecture owns the opening frame.
- A central axis and horizontal booking ribbon turn composition into interaction.
- Lacquer black, deep green, warm ivory, service paper, and rare gold define the material world.
- Squared controls, crisp separators, and ledger-like service rows keep the system precise.
- Conceptual service imagery is clearly disclosed and delivered as compressed WebP assets.
- Motion is restrained, directional, and removed when reduced motion is preferred.

## Colors

The palette is dusk-toned and architectural: dark grounds carry real imagery, warm ivory and service paper create editorial reading surfaces, and gold acts as a scarce ceremonial signal.

### Primary

- **Ceremonial Gold:** Marks primary actions, active links, field labels, selection, alignment rules, and key borders. Its warmth connects the interface to the venue's evening lighting.
- **Action Gold:** Fills the principal date-check action; the lighter hover value supplies a clear, contained state change.

### Secondary

- **Deep Courtyard Green:** Grounds planning and contact surfaces and closes the services ledger, carrying the venue's outdoor character beyond the hero photograph.
- **Courtyard Green:** Appears as the brighter green reference where green must remain legible against gold or light surfaces.

### Tertiary

- **Service Link Bronze:** Carries text actions on pale service panels, providing a deeper and more readable counterpart to ceremonial gold.

### Neutral

- **Night Lacquer:** The dominant canvas, primary ink on gold and paper, booking-ribbon surface, and dark button fill.
- **Masthead Black:** Deepens navigation, image-disclosure labels, and footer bands so they frame rather than compete with imagery.
- **Warm Ivory:** The default light text and the services section's outer paper-like field.
- **Hero Ivory:** Gives the opening headline a slightly warmer, brighter presence over the photograph.
- **Service Paper:** Separates ledger entries from the slightly darker warm-ivory section field without creating floating cards.
- **Ledger Rule:** Forms the one-pixel services grid, internal list divider, and other warm-surface separations.
- **Service Soft Ink:** Supports explanatory copy on service paper while preserving clear hierarchy beneath night-lacquer headings.

### Named Rules

**The Gold Is Geometry Rule.** Use gold for alignment, action, threshold, and emphasis—not as ambient decoration across an entire surface.

**The Real Place Rule.** Dark overlays may improve legibility, but they must preserve the recognizable venue, its lighting, and its central architectural axis.

**The Evidence Label Rule.** Conceptual imagery must carry a visible "Visual direction" label and a nearby section disclosure; it never inherits the evidentiary status of authentic venue photography.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Akatab (with sans-serif fallback)  
**Body Font:** Manrope (with sans-serif fallback)  
**Brand Accent Font:** Georgia (with serif fallback), limited to the compact brand-name treatment

**Character:** Akatab supplies broad, humanist authority for large statements, service titles, and actions; Manrope keeps navigation, forms, disclosures, and explanatory copy clean and contemporary. Georgia is a contained legacy accent around the existing identity, never the page's editorial voice.

The machine-readable frontmatter records both semantic roles and every intentional endpoint in the responsive type scale. Fluid roles remain tied to their actual contexts—hero, services, enquiry, contact, planning, navigation, and booking controls—while the enumerated scale lets tooling validate compact labels and responsive overrides without flattening them into one generic text size.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** (700, fluid 52–102px, 0.98 line-height): Major dark-section statements; balance line breaks and keep measures compact.
- **Service Display** (700, fluid 58–96px, 0.98 line-height): The services-ledger introduction on wide screens, scaled to 40–46px with a 1.0 line-height at mobile widths.
- **Headline** (700, fluid 44–75px, 0.94 line-height): The hero statement, set tightly enough to feel architectural over the image; mobile resolves to 40–48px with a 0.96 line-height.
- **Title** (700, fluid 24–32px, 1 line-height): Planning steps and compact section introductions.
- **Service Title** (700, fluid 32–54px, 1–1.04 line-height): Featured and standard ledger titles; all service titles settle at 32px with a 1.04 line-height on mobile.
- **Body** (400, fluid 17–21px, 1.72 line-height): Explanatory content, generally held to 48–62 characters for comfortable reading.
- **Mobile Body** (400, 16px, 1.65–1.68 line-height): Services, event, and enquiry copy below 640px; its tighter rhythm offsets the single-column page length without reducing readability.
- **Label** (700, 13px, 0.06em tracking, uppercase): Enquiry-field labels and operational metadata.
- **Action** (700, fluid 16–25px, 1 line-height): The booking ribbon's decisive gold action.

### Named Rules

**The Humanist Authority Rule.** Use Akatab for authored statements and consequential actions; use Manrope for everything people must scan, read, disclose, or enter.

**The Serif Containment Rule.** Serif type belongs only to the compact brand-name expression associated with the supplied logo; do not turn the site into a cream-serif wedding template.

**The Mobile Presence Rule.** At 640px and below, reduce display scale and leading together, keep service titles at 32px, and preserve a 16px body floor rather than miniaturizing the desktop hierarchy.

## Layout

The site uses an architectural editorial grid capped at 1672px. The opening viewport follows the approved 1672:941 composition: a 10% masthead, an 80% image field, a vertical centerline, a booking ribbon crossing the image, and a 20% planning-band glimpse. The ribbon is inset 4.4% and divides into 24% / 24% / 27% / 25% columns so the action carries the strongest endpoint.

Full-page sections alternate two-column editorial arrangements, generally at ratios near 1:1 or 0.8:1.2, with fluid horizontal gutters and deep vertical rhythm. The services area is a bordered ledger rather than a card collection: its featured wedding entry spans both columns, Valima and private-gathering entries share the next row, and the deep-green planning band spans the full width. One-pixel rules, not gaps or shadows, make the cells legible.

At 980px, major two-column section layouts stack, including the featured service and planning band, while the two standard service entries may remain paired. At 900px, navigation becomes a toggled vertical menu, the architectural axis is removed, and the booking ribbon becomes a one-column stack within a fixed-height hero composition. At 640px, outer gutters become 20px, service/event/enquiry blocks use 72px vertical padding, the services ledger becomes one column, service imagery uses a 4:3 frame, service copy uses 24px inline padding, forms become single-column, and contact/footer groups stack.

**The Axis-to-Stack Rule.** Preserve the fountain-aligned composition on wide screens; below 900px, replace it with a clear vertical sequence rather than compressing the axis.

**The Ledger-to-Column Rule.** Preserve shared borders and reading order when the two-column services ledger becomes a single mobile column; do not split its entries into floating cards.

## Elevation & Depth

This is a flat-by-default system. Depth comes primarily from authentic photographic light, tonal section changes, overlay shade, and fine borders. The booking ribbon receives one structural shadow so it reads as a working control crossing the photograph; service entries, fields, and content sections remain planar. Focus is contrast-aware: warm-ivory outlines appear on dark surfaces, while services and enquiry controls use night-lacquer outlines on their light surfaces.

### Shadow Vocabulary

- **Hero Shade** (`inset 29rem 0 12rem rgba(2, 5, 5, .5), inset 0 -9rem 8rem rgba(2, 9, 9, .2)`): Protects hero-copy contrast while preserving the lit facade.
- **Booking Lift** (`0 18px 34px rgba(0, 0, 0, .28)`): Separates the functional booking ribbon from venue imagery.
- **Field Focus Underline** (`0 2px 0 #0b1110`): Reinforces focused enquiry fields on gold without rounding or glow.
- **Dark-Surface Focus** (`2px solid #e8e0d1`, 4px offset): Makes keyboard focus explicit on lacquer and green surfaces.
- **Light-Surface Focus** (`2px solid #0b1110`, 4px offset): Reverses the focus outline over warm-ivory, service-paper, and gold surfaces for dependable contrast.

### Named Rules

**The Architecture Before Shadow Rule.** Establish depth with image, tone, and rules first; reserve cast shadow for the booking ribbon and use outlines or underlines for focus response.

**The Surface-Contrast Focus Rule.** Focus color follows the surface beneath it: warm ivory on dark material, night lacquer on light material, always with a visible offset where an outline is used.

## Shapes

The form language is square-cut and rectilinear. Buttons, fields, ribbons, service-ledger cells, disclosure labels, navigation actions, captions, and section boundaries use zero corner radius. One-pixel borders and dividers create rhythm; image containers clip cleanly to their grid without ornamental masks.

**The Square-Cut Rule.** New interactive controls use square corners unless a supplied identity asset carries its own intrinsic silhouette.

## Components

### Buttons

- **Shape:** Square and architectural (0 radius), generally bordered or full-bleed within a grid cell.
- **Date Check:** Night ink on action gold, set in bold Akatab and occupying the ribbon's entire endpoint; hover shifts to the lighter gold.
- **Enquiry Submit:** Warm ivory on night lacquer, 72px tall with a spaced arrow; hover reverses to transparent gold with night ink.
- **Header Action:** Gold text and a one-pixel gold outline on masthead black; hover fills gold and reverses to night ink.
- **Focus:** Use the two-pixel, four-pixel-offset treatment appropriate to the surface: warm ivory on dark sections and night lacquer on the light services/enquiry sections.

### Cards / Containers

- **Corner Style:** Square (0 radius).
- **Background:** Tonal blocks—night lacquer, deep courtyard green, warm ivory, service paper, or gold—rather than floating neutral cards.
- **Shadow Strategy:** Flat at rest; the booking ribbon alone uses Booking Lift.
- **Border:** One-pixel gold, ivory-alpha, stone, or ledger-rule strokes depending on surrounding contrast.
- **Internal Padding:** Desktop service copy is fluid at 34–74px; mobile service copy uses 30px top, 24px inline, and 34px bottom, while the green planning cell uses 38px top, 24px inline, and 42px bottom.

### Inputs / Fields

- **Booking Ribbon:** Dark filled cells with gold labels, warm-ivory values, 20px line icons, and gold separators. On mobile, each cell becomes at least 72px tall and separators move to the bottom edge.
- **Enquiry Form:** Transparent fields on gold with a single dark underline, square corners, and 14px top / 16px bottom padding.
- **Shared State:** Event type, preferred date, and estimated guests remain synchronized between the booking ribbon and the enquiry form.
- **Service Preselection:** Wedding, Valima, and Private gathering service actions set the matching event type before moving to the enquiry form; the planning CTA leaves the visitor's current selection intact.
- **Focus:** Enquiry fields deepen their underline with the Field Focus Underline treatment; services and enquiry keyboard targets use the light-surface night-lacquer outline.
- **Error / Disabled:** No bespoke error or disabled visual state is currently established; preserve semantic browser behavior until these states are deliberately designed.

### Navigation

Desktop navigation sits directly on masthead black in Manrope with warm-ivory links and a gold hover. The outlined action remains visually distinct at the right edge. Below 900px, the brand mark, action, and square menu toggle remain visible while links open in a full-width black ledger with ivory-alpha dividers.

### Booking Ribbon

The signature component binds the venue's central fountain axis to a functional event-type, date, guest-count, and action sequence. It must read as one precise horizontal instrument on desktop—not four floating cards—and as one ordered vertical form on mobile. Its entrance reveals outward from the center while the vertical axis grows downward; both animations yield to reduced-motion preference.

### Services Ledger

The services ledger is the light-surface counterpart to the booking ribbon: one continuous square-edged grid with a full-width featured service, two standard service cells, and a full-width deep-green planning close. Images and copy alternate evidence and action rather than behaving as interchangeable cards. On mobile, the ledger becomes an ordered image-copy sequence with 4:3 media and deliberate 32px titles.

Each service image is a lazy-loaded compressed WebP asset. Because these images are generated visual directions rather than verified completed-event photography, each media frame carries a dark "Visual direction" disclosure with a restrained gold border, and the ledger ends with a plain-language disclosure that states their conceptual status and directs final inclusions and availability back to the venue team.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** let authentic venue architecture and recognizable imagery carry the primary visual claim.
- **Do** use ceremonial gold sparingly for actions, rules, active affordances, and alignment.
- **Do** preserve square controls, one-pixel separators, large humanist statements, and ledger-like information structures.
- **Do** keep services as one connected editorial ledger: full-width feature, paired standard entries, and a green planning close.
- **Do** stack complex desktop compositions clearly at 980px and 900px, then use 20px gutters, 72px section padding, 32px service titles, and 16px body copy at 640px.
- **Do** preselect the service's event type before carrying a visitor into the enquiry form.
- **Do** preserve visible disclosure on conceptual imagery, lazy-load service media, ship its compressed WebP form, and retain metadata alongside the assets.
- **Do** provide contrast-aware keyboard focus and honor reduced-motion preference.

### Don't:

- **Don't** add generic luxury flourishes, bridal ornament, purple gradients, pervasive glass effects, or pill-heavy UI.
- **Don't** convert the booking ribbon, services ledger, planning band, or contact rows into rounded floating cards.
- **Don't** use gold as a full-page ambient wash outside the established enquiry section or as decoration without meaning.
- **Don't** present conceptual service imagery as verified venue evidence or remove its "Visual direction" labels and section disclosure.
- **Don't** substitute heavyweight raster formats for the established compressed WebP service assets without a measured quality or compatibility reason.
- **Don't** invent visualized pricing, capacity, packages, ratings, testimonials, or statistics.
